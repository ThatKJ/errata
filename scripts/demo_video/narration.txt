s1|Errata. When a product fact changes, it finds the customer answers you already sent that are now wrong, and drafts a correction for a human to approve. The narration in this video is synthesized speech.
s2|Everything here is synthetic: a fictional company, Kestrel Ledger, and fictional customers. These thirty answers were drafted with Hindsight reflect, and each one stores the facts it cited, from reflect's based-on field. Answer Q02 told an Indian microfinance customer that its data could not be hosted in India.
s3|Now a live call, on this laptop. A note from legal says Snowflake is a new subprocessor. Errata retains the note in the local Hindsight server, then Hindsight recall ranks which fact it supersedes.
s3b|The subprocessor fact, F03, comes first. A human confirms before anything changes. I won't apply it in this recording, because checking every linked answer takes several minutes of local model time.
s4|This change was verified earlier, and is shown from saved results. The Mumbai region launch superseded fact F01. The old version is invalidated in Hindsight, not deleted. Twelve sent answers were linked to it: one needs correction, nine need human review, and two are still accurate. A verdict of needs correction must quote the sentence that became false.
s5|The negative control is Q03: a US credit union told its data stays in the US. That is still true. In this run it was not marked still accurate; it went to human review instead. It was not flagged as wrong either.
s6|The evidence time machine for Q02. Fact version one. The answer as sent, with the false sentence highlighted. The change, with the old memory read live from Hindsight as invalidated, with its reason and timestamp. The new version. The verdict and its based-on memories. And the human decision.
s7|For an answer that needs review, the drafted correction is editable, and the reviewer's name is recorded. Approving writes a new answer version and retains the decision in Hindsight. Nothing is ever sent to a customer. I don't approve here. Q02's correction was approved earlier, and the audit trail records it.
s8|How it held up. On thirty synthetic answers and four changes, with twelve stale answers labelled in advance, none were silently cleared, in two runs. Precision of needs correction was only 0.55. Four of the five false alarms quoted a sentence that really was stale. Recall picked the right fact four times out of four. Each verification takes about thirty-eight seconds with a local qwen model, so it runs as a background job. One synthetic dataset and one model: a mechanism test, not an accuracy claim.
